# Patterns to Minimize

Composition editing reference. Not a ban list or lexical cleanup pass. Keep a pattern when it is clearest, most precise, or natural for reader and genre. Rewrite only when a clearer alternative exists.

Add entries only for recurring patterns that weaken reader understanding. Each entry: signal, cost, preferred rewrite, exceptions.

## Identity drift through synonym rotation

- **Signal:** One actor or thing receives changing names to avoid repetition: `user`, `customer`, `client`.
- **Cost:** Reader cannot tell whether terms mean one entity or several.
- **Prefer:** Use one accurate name throughout a local passage. Change it only to mark a real distinction or necessary legal, technical, or relational role.
- **Exception:** Deliberate contrast: customer buys; user operates; client has a service relationship.

## Empty hedging

- **Signal:** Stacked hedges, throat-clearing, or modal verbs obscure a claim: `It is important to note that this may potentially help`.
- **Cost:** More words, no stated consequence or confidence level.
- **Prefer:** State action, outcome, and actual uncertainty: `This may reduce checkout delays.`
- **Exception:** Keep calibrated uncertainty when evidence, forecast, permission, or responsibility requires it.

## Nominalized verbs

- **Signal:** Weak verb plus abstract noun replaces a direct verb: `perform an analysis of`.
- **Cost:** Inflates prose and hides action.
- **Prefer:** Use direct verb: `analyze`.
- **Exception:** Keep noun when it names a defined process, artifact, metric, or concept: `the analysis supports...`.

## Overloaded sentences

- **Signal:** Several independent ideas stitched into one sentence, especially with repeated em dashes, commas, or clauses.
- **Cost:** Reader loses sequence, responsibility, or consequence.
- **Prefer:** One main claim or action per sentence. Split independent ideas; use punctuation to show real relationship.
- **Exception:** Keep deliberate long sentence when its structure stays easy to follow and its rhythm serves reader.
